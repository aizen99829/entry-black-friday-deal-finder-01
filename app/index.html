Is it really a deal? How the verdict works

A big red “was” price is not proof of a bargain. Some stores show a “comparable value” that the item never actually sold at, and some “discounts” are a few dollars on a bundle you would buy anyway. So every deal here is scored from its numbers, the same way for all 24:

  • Real deal — 40% off or more. The sale price is the story, not the sticker.
  • Fair deal — 15% to 39% off. A genuine saving, but not a once-a-year price.
  • Thin deal — under 15% off. Only worth it if you already wanted exactly this item.

Where the “was” price is the store’s own reference number rather than a price the item was seen selling at, the card says so — the percentage is still shown, but the verdict is honest about what it rests on.

  • What this is not: prices change hourly in Black Friday week and stock runs out. Treat every price as “seen at this time”, and check the store page before paying. Apple’s own event (Nov 27–30) gives gift cards, not price cuts — that is why no Apple Store deal is listed.
