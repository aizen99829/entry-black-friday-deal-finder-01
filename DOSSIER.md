## The verdict rule ("is it really a deal?")

Computed from the numbers, never written by hand, the same rule in
`main.ts` (smoke check) and `app/app.js` (the page):

- **Real deal — 40% off or more.** The sale price is the story.
- **Fair deal — 15% to 39% off.** A genuine saving, not a once-a-year price.
- **Thin deal — under 15% off.** Only worth it if you already wanted exactly
  this item. Two deals land here on purpose: the Switch 2 bundle (9%, the
  first cut ever on the bundle — worth knowing, not worth chasing) and the
  Mac mini M6 (13%).

A finder that calls everything a "Real deal" is an advert. This one keeps
all three verdicts so the visitor can tell the difference.

## Honesty notes — where the numbers are weaker than they look

- **Best Buy "comparable value" (deals 1–2).** The $2,099.99 / $1,999.99
  figures are Best Buy's own "comp. value", not prices these TVs were seen
  selling at. Both cards say so, and both verdicts would still be Real on a
  much lower reference price ($999.99 is a genuine doorbuster price for a
  2026 OLED either way).
- **TCL QM8CG (deal 5).** Best Buy printed "Save 70%, only $599.99" without
  printing the regular price; $1,999.99 is implied by the 70% figure. The
  card says the regular price is implied, not printed.
- **Blink bundle (deal 20).** The $150 is the bundle's own list price (three
  cameras plus a doorbell), not a single camera's price. The card says so.
- **AirPods Pro 3 (deal 6).** Amazon's $149 is the lowest reported
  ($149.99 at Target and Best Buy, read live the same day). The $149 figure
  comes from 9to5Toys' 23 Nov report, not from a page read on the 27th —
  the card's verification text says exactly that.
- **Conflicting reports, resolved conservatively.** Where reports disagreed
  (e.g. MacBook Air M5 at $1,049 vs a $989 sighting; Apple Watch Series 12
  at $299 vs $399), the entry lists the price corroborated by two or more
  sources and leaves the uncorroborated one out. The Watch Series 12 was
  left out entirely for that reason.
